# 3D clips

Read this before any clip with a 3D camera: subspaces, planes and their normals, surfaces, fields in space. It is
[clips.md](clips.md) plus what a tilted camera changes. The worked example is `scenes/math309/four_subspaces_3d.py`;
the gallery has one short demo of each technique below (`gallery-plane-and-line`, `gallery-orbit-stop`,
`gallery-matrix-3d`, `gallery-parametric-surface`, `gallery-potential-surface`, `gallery-vector-field-3d`).

## The scene: `MnScene3D`

```python
from mn_style import *
from amanim import oblique_view, edge_on_view, plane_patch, vec3

class Subspaces(MnScene3D):
    def construct(self):
        n = np.array([1.0, 1.0, 1.0])
        home = oblique_view(n)                       # computed: the plane shows as a plane
        self.set_camera_orientation(*home, zoom=0.9)
        ax, names = self.axes(("x_1", "x_2", "x_3"))
        inset = self.pin(small_diagram().to_corner(UL))   # an overlay that ignores the camera
        plane = plane_patch(n, color=ACCENT)
        lab = self.label("R(A^T)", 2.2 * plane.b2, color=ACCENT)   # faces the camera wherever it goes
        self.play(Create(ax), FadeIn(names), FadeIn(inset), FadeIn(plane), FadeIn(lab))
        self.note(r"Each row sums to 0, so $R(A^T)\perp(1,1,1)$.")  # pinned to the frame
```

- **`MnScene3D`** is `MnScene` + `ThreeDScene`, in that order, so the camera is a `ThreeDCamera` and the layout check
  still runs. Don't write `class X(MnScene, ThreeDScene)` yourself.
- **`self.note()`** pins its caption to the frame. A caption in world space would be drawn lying in the floor.
- **`self.pin(m, ...)`** fixes overlays to the frame: an inset diagram, a heading, an equation beside the picture.
  Pinned mobjects draw above everything.
- **`self.label(text, at, color, plate=False)`** puts a label at a 3D point. It always faces the camera and moves with
  the point. Put it past a vector's tip along the vector, or off a line's end, so no view stacks it on another label.
  `plate=True` backs it when it sits on a plane's grid.
- **`self.axes(names)`** gives quiet axes with no ticks and facing labels. Edge-on views stack two axis labels on
  one line: fade those out for the edge-on beat.
- `pin`, `label`, and `axes`' labels come back registered but not shown: `FadeIn` them.
- **The layout check sees what the camera draws.** Labels are projected through the camera before they're tested
  for overlaps, pinned things count as screen-space, and `keep_clear(line)` tests a label against the projected
  line. Keep the lines and arrows labels must not touch in `keep_clear`, including a `split_parallelogram`.
- **The camera starts flat** (phi=0, theta=-90°), so a 2D section (a title, a matrix, a chain of equations) works as
  in `MnScene`. Put it back with `self.set_camera_orientation(phi=0, theta=-90 * DEGREES, zoom=1)`.

## Compute the view; never guess it

A guessed "nice" angle can look along the very thing it's meant to show. The four-subspaces chat's first view (phi
68°, theta −62°) lay inside the row space, so the plane drew as a line, and the gallery's first try at a 3×3 matrix
drew two of its columns on one screen line, so the parallelepiped looked flat. amanim's views (`amanim.threed`) return
`View(phi, theta)`; pass them as `self.set_camera_orientation(*view, zoom=0.85)` or `self.move_camera(*view)`.

| You want | Call |
|---|---|
| A plane that reads as a plane, its normal line still long | `oblique_view(normal)`: 54° off the normal |
| The same, and these vectors drawn at a readable length and apart | `oblique_view(normal, show=[x, x_r, x_n])` |
| The plane edge-on, and the right angle between its normal and a line in it drawn as 90° | `edge_on_view(normal, line_dir)` |
| Several vectors (a basis and where a matrix sends it) with no two stacked on one screen line | `clear_view(*vectors)` |
| An orbit that ends exactly on a computed view | `move_camera(phi=v.phi, theta=orbit_to(theta_now, v.theta, turns=1))` |
| A label moved on screen by a set amount (a label on its own line: x_n lies on N(A)) | `self.label(t, p + nudge(view, right=0.3, up=-0.4))` |
| To check a view | `facing(normal, view)` (1 face-on, 0 edge-on), `apparent_angle(u, v, view)` |

The shapes are VMobjects, cheap to move the camera around: `plane_patch(normal, size, color=...)` (with the grid that
makes a tilt read; `.b1` is its level direction, `.b2` the other), `line_through(dir, length)`, `right_angle_3d(line,
plane_dir)`, `vec3(v, start)`, `split_parallelogram(v, a, b)` (`.whole`, `.a`, `.b`, `.sides`), `wireframe` and
`height_wireframe` (instead of a Surface), `vector_field_3d(F, points)`, `axes_3d`.

Labels:

- **Let `self.place(text, at, toward=..., avoid=[...])` put them.** It tries spots around the point and
  keeps the first that the layout check would call clear: in the frame, off other text (including labels placed
  but not shown yet), and off kept-clear shapes. `toward` (a vector's own direction) puts a label past its tip.
  Call it after the camera is set: the spot is judged from that view. `gallery-label-placement` shows it.
- A label is pinned to one 3D point, so a placement that is clear in one view can land on a line in another. When a
  view stacks labels (edge-on views do), fade the stacked ones for that beat, and give a line's label a second copy
  placed for the edge-on view if it needs one there.
- Past a vector's tip along the vector (`1.3 * v`), off a line's end, or `nudge`d off a line it lies on.
- `plate=True` for a label on a plane's grid.

## What a 3D clip costs to render

A chat's clip renders at 720p30 and gets 870 s (`scripts/render_runner.py`). In a `ThreeDScene`, **while the camera
moves (a `move_camera`, an ambient rotation) every mobject is redrawn on every frame**; while it's still, only what's
animating is. So the cost of a 3D clip is its camera time multiplied by what's on screen.

Measured on the Obelisk in the sandbox, 720p30, camera moving (`scenes/bench/cost_3d.py`, 2026-10-23). Seconds of
rendering per second of video:

| On screen while the camera moves | s / video s |
|---|---|
| nothing (encoding alone) | 0.2–0.4 |
| 9 `vec3` arrows (Line + crossed tip), 9 `Line`s, 9 labels, or one `plane_patch` | ~0.25 each group |
| `wireframe` sphere 12×12, `height_wireframe` 15×15 | 0.35 |
| `vector_field_3d`, 64 arrows | 0.65 |
| ManimCE `ThreeDAxes` (no ticks) | 1.9 |
| 9 `Line3D` | 6.2 |
| `Surface` 16×16 / 32×32 | 3.7 / 13.4 |
| **9 `Arrow3D`** (resolution 6 or default: the cone ignores it) | **104–108** |

| The same busy picture (axes, a plane, 9 arrows, 9 labels) | s / video s |
|---|---|
| a static `wait` | 0.23 |
| a `wait` with one updater moving one dot | 0.33 |
| one arrow drawn in, camera still | 0.33 |
| ambient camera rotation | 2.1 |

And each distinct `MathTex`/`Tex` string compiles once per render, about 0.3 s each: a clip with 40 captions spends
12 s on LaTeX.

Rules of thumb:

- **Never `Arrow3D`.** One costs about 12 s per second of camera motion: nine of them during 10 s of moves is over
  1,000 s, past the cap. Use `vec3` (amanim), which reads from every angle at a Line's cost.
- **No `Surface` for a picture you'll orbit.** Use `wireframe` / `height_wireframe`. A `Surface` 16×16 at most, and
  only for a still camera.
- **No `Line3D` or `ThreeDAxes`.** Use `line_through` and `self.axes()` (amanim's `axes_3d`).
- **Budget camera time, not clip time.** A still camera is nearly free; a move costs everything on screen. Built
  from the cheap pieces, the four-subspaces clip (131 s of video, 4 camera moves, 13 sections)
  renders at 720p30 in 87 s on the Obelisk: well under 1 s of render per second of video. The chat's original,
  with Arrow3D, ThreeDAxes, and an ambient rotation, took 909 s for the same content and had to be split in two.
- **Ambient rotation is a camera move that never stops.** Use a short `move_camera` to a computed view instead; it
  costs the same per second and ends where you choose.
- **Updaters during camera moves are cheap.** The move already redraws everything; an updater adds only its own
  arithmetic.
- **Draft at `-ql` first.** 480p15 is about a quarter of the pixels and half the frames. `render.py --hq` times a
  clip at the runner's 720p30 without publishing it.

## What 3D can't show

Check each before you publish a 3D clip, and say the true thing in a caption where the picture can't. These came out of
the four-subspaces clip, where the picture struggled or quietly lied. After he watches, they make good probes: ask the
question, and let him answer before you explain.

| The picture | What's true | Probe to ask after |
|---|---|---|
| An edge-on plane looks like a line. | A projection can hide a dimension; the plane is still 2D. | "In the edge-on view R(Aᵀ) looked like a line. Is it one? What dimension is it?" |
| Right angles look right only from special views. | The angle is 90° in every view; only its drawing changes. | "From the first view the corner looked like 40°. Was N(A) ⟂ R(Aᵀ) there?" |
| Node space and edge space look identical. | They are two different ℝ³'s; the clip says which one it's in. | "Which space does (1,1,−1) live in, and what does each coordinate mean?" |
| A can't be animated from one to the other. | A map ℝ³ → ℝ³ between two spaces would need 6D; the clip shows one example, x and Ax. | "Why does the clip show x and then Ax in a new picture, instead of moving x to Ax?" |
| The "driven + loop" split of y looks orthogonal. | It's orthogonal only with equal resistors; otherwise in the C⁻¹ inner product. Tellegen (yᵀAx = 0) needs only A. | "With R1 ≠ R2, which of these still holds: y_c ⟂ y_loop, or yᵀ(Ax) = 0?" |
| Translucent planes overlap. | Depth and which is in front are ambiguous; rely on labels and the edge-on view, not shading. | "In the split, was y_c in front of the plane or behind it? How would you tell?" |
| Drawn planes have corners. | A subspace has no edges; the patch is a window onto it. Keep its edges outside the action. | "Where does R(A) end?" |
| Bigger graphs leave 3D. | Anything past a triangle graph has ℝⁿ and ℝᵐ with n, m > 3; only the counts carry over. | "For 4 nodes and 5 edges, what are the four dimensions?" |
| N(AᵀCA) = N(A) had no picture. | AᵀCA x = 0 ⟹ xᵀAᵀCAx = ‖C^½Ax‖² = 0 ⟹ Ax = 0 (C positive). | "Why does grounding one node make AᵀCA invertible?" |

Don't turn these into cards on your own: ask him first.
